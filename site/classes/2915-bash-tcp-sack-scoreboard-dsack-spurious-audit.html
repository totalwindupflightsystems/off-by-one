Root cause (why the naive audit fails)

ss -ti counters (retrans, lost, reord_seen, dsack_dups) are cumulative, per-socket, and cannot be tied to a sequence range. Deciding "loss vs reordering vs spurious RTO" requires an explicit sender-side scoreboard (SND.UNA + SACK ranges) rebuilt from the ACK/SACK stream, plus time-correlation of D-SACK blocks with retransmission events. Two traps overrule otherwise-convincing evidence:

  • A D-SACK below the cumulative ACK proves the retransmission was unnecessary even though the data is no longer in the scoreboard.
  • Reneging: a previously SACKed block that is later omitted and then retransmitted without a D-SACK voids the old SACK evidence and must be scored as loss, not reordering.

Only two RFC 2883 DSACK cases are machine-detectable from the wire (matching Linux tcp_check_dsack()): first block starts below ACK, or first block is contained in the second block. A lone repeated SACK must not be treated as a D-SACK.
