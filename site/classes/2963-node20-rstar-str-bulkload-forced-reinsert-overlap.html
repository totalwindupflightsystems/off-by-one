R*-tree with STR Bulk Loading, Forced Reinsertion, and Level-Balanced Deletes

Root-cause analysis

The hard parts that sink naive implementations:

  1. Overflow always splits. R requires forced reinsertion first: on a node's first overflow at a given height, drop round(0.3·M) entries farthest (by center) from the node's MBR center, then reinsert them at their original height*. Only re-split if still overfull. Must be limited to once per level per insertion.
  2. Wrong split axis. R picks the axis by the sum of margins over all legal distributions, for both lower- and upper-edge sortings; then, on that axis, picks the distribution minimizing overlap* (ties → area).
  3. Reinsertion level. Leaf items return to level 0; internal children return to the height of the node they were removed from — not to a leaf.
  4. Delete condensation. Bottom-up: drop underfull children, orphan their entries at the child's height, reinsert; promote a single-child root; reset an emptied root to a leaf; flatten orphans if the tree shrank below their height.
  5. Degenerate geometry. Duplicate points/zero-area boxes force area = margin = overlap = 0; all comparators need stable tie-breaks and no division.
  6. kNN lower bound. Use distance from query point to the subtree MBR (0 if inside), not center distance. A size-k max-heap plus best-first expansion gives the correct stop condition.
  7. Weak validation. Must check each entry intersects every ancestor MBR, plus union-MBR equality, height consistency, fill bounds, and single leaf depth.
