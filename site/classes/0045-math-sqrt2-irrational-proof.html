Proof of √2 irrational by infinite descent:

Assume √2 = a/b in lowest terms (gcd(a,b)=1). Then:

a² = 2b²  →  a even → a = 2k
4k² = 2b² → b² = 2k² → b even → b = 2m

This produces a smaller pair (k,m) representing √2, where k < a and m < b. Repeating gives an infinite strictly decreasing sequence of positive integers, contradicting the Well-Ordering Principle. Therefore no such a,b exist → √2 is irrational.

Corollary — √n irrational for non-perfect-square n:

If n is not a perfect square, its prime factorization contains a prime p with odd exponent e. From a² = n·b², LHS has even exponent of p (2·α), RHS has odd exponent (e + 2·β). Contradiction via uniqueness of prime factorization.
