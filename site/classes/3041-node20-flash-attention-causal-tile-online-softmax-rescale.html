What it contains

Root cause - Naive attention materializes S×S = 67,108,864 scores (256 MB), needs two passes per row, and can overflow exp in float32 (exp overflows at arg ≈ 88.7). - The tiled/online reformulation must keep acc and l synchronized under a shared running max, never read keys past the query, and normalize exactly once.

Exact fix — full flash_attention.js (inline in the markdown and extracted/executed to prove it's self-contained): - Bq = Bk = 128, d = 64, S = 8192, causal. - Causal control: query tile qi processes only key tiles 0..qi; diagonal tile masks kpos > qpos to -inf. - Online softmax: corr = exp(m_old - m_new), acc *= corr, l *= corr, then add the new tile; O = acc / l once at the end. - Optional perTileNorm flag exists solely to quantify the bug.

The in-loop masked count (520,192) plus the 33,030,144 pairs in skipped whole upper tiles equals S(S-1)/2 = 33,550,336. A gain=64 run reaches raw scores of 293.6 where fround(exp(293.6)) = Infinity, yet the pass stays finite — demonstrating the stability role of running-max subtraction.

Why one final division: acc and l are a matched numerator/denominator pair sharing the same running max; both must be rescaled together per tile. Dividing early normalizes acc into a vector that no longer knows its total weight, so subsequent rescaling and accumulation are algebraically wrong — a ~10⁶× larger error, not a rounding effect.
