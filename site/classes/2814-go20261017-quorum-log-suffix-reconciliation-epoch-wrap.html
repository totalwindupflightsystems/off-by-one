Root cause

The failure is the intersection of four independent hazards:

  1. Wrapped terms aren’t a total order. With a uint32 counter, a > b and even a == b are wrong across a wrap; two different entries can share the same raw counter.
  2. Snapshots erase the join point. Peers expose only (snapshotIndex, snapshotTerm) plus a suffix, so there is no shared array index to compare from.
  3. Acks arrive out of order. A delayed reply can regress matchIndex/nextIndex, and a reply from a previous wrap epoch can impersonate the current one.
  4. Committed safety vs. minimal transfer. The cheapest repair keeps the longest prefix; the safe repair never rewrites committed entries without proof.

The fix

  • TermResolver compares counters by the half‑modulus shortest‑congruent delta, anchored at the leader term; exactly half a modulus ⇒ ErrAmbiguousTerm (insufficient metadata).
  • WireStamp{Epoch, Counter} + UnwrapSequence/ResolveRaw give a genuinely total order for production wire formats.
  • Reconcile from base = max(local.snapshot, remote.snapshot) upward, keeping the longest provably‑equal prefix; emit a plan {SendSnapshot, TruncateFrom, Entries}.
  • Distinguish ErrCommittedConflict (proven mismatch at/below commit) from ErrInsufficientMetadata (probe gap over a required committed rewrite) — never guess.
  • AckTracker tags requests with a never‑wrapping Seq, makes success idempotent via max, drops superseded rejections, and refuses ambiguous wrapped‑term replies.
